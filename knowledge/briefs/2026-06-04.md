# Morning Brief — 4 June 2026

VN-Index closed at 1,831.55, up 0.69% on 601M shares — healthy volume, breadth held up. The headline number flatters: HNX fell 3.98% on the same session, a sharp divergence that signals small-cap and mid-tier names were distributed hard while large-caps carried the index. Both proprietary desks and foreign investors were net sellers on the day [[cafef-thi-truong-ck_2026-06-04_7cfe748c]], meaning the VN-Index gain was driven by price, not flow. The standout mover was PLX: ceiling (+6.90%, 6.4M shares), sparked by the board resolving to sell all 23.3M treasury shares to meet the public company free-float threshold [[cafef-thi-truong-ck_2026-06-04_3b091a05]]. Treasury share supply coming to market is ordinarily a headwind, but the market read it as clearing a regulatory overhang — smart positioning ahead of the actual sale.

Watchlist is empty. This matters for MWG holders — Điện Máy Xanh is the core retail asset; a credible institutional anchor at IPO reprices the sum-of-parts upward and removes the liquidity discount that suppresses MWG's current multiple. Watch for the IPO price range announcement; Dragon Capital's floor bid sets the lower bound.

Today's theme is valuation in the context of IPO price discovery. The Berkshire principle is blunt: *"The disposition of money unmasks humans."* [[berkshire-hathaway-letters_2026-06-04_e792dfa9]] Dragon Capital writing a $50M+ cheque into a pre-public company is not a passive allocation — it is a conviction bet on a price they believe the market has not yet found. The thing to remember: cornerstone investors in VN IPOs rarely lose money over a 12-month horizon when the anchor is an institution of that calibre. Their entry price is the thesis.
